Particle Whale

Drag to turn · tap the whale to ripple · tap the water to scatter

Whale

Two whales, built from shapes and then dusted with dots. The blue whale’s mouth runs back along the side of its head; when it opens you see the baleen hanging from the upper jaw and the pleated throat below. The sperm whale is Moby Dick: a boxy head and a narrow toothed jaw. X-ray turns the skin to glass and shows the skeleton: skull, mandibles, about 56 vertebrae, ribs, and the arm and finger bones inside each flipper. Ink on paper turns the shading around: dots gather in the shadows and along the outline, like an engraving.

How it’s built

1 · Shape: the body is a row of rounded cross-sections from snout to tail (boxy at the head, round in the middle, flat-sided at the tail), joined into a skin of triangles. Jaw, flippers, flukes, baleen and every bone are made the same way. 2 · Skin: each triangle gets a tone: wrinkles on the forehead and flanks, a few old scars, the eye. 3 · Dots: a triangle’s chance of getting a dot is its area × its tone — the same running-total trick as the painting, on a surface instead of a picture. 4 · Light: every frame, each dot asks how much it faces the light and the camera.

Dots

Light

Outline lights the dots seen edge-on. Solid hides the far side; at 0 the whale is glass and you see through to its other flank.

Camera

Dots away from the focus grow into soft discs and dim, so the light they carry stays the same. Focus − is nearer, + is farther.

Motion

Swim across: the whale crosses the picture and leaves it, then comes back from somewhere else. Stay here keeps it in the middle to turn around. The lower jaw is long and narrow and swings open on a hinge under the eye, showing the only teeth a sperm whale has. Whales swim by beating the tail up and down, so the wave runs along the back toward the flukes. Tap the whale and the dots leap off its skin in rings that run out from that spot; tap the water around it and the dots scatter. Sea = the share of dots left drifting as marine snow.
